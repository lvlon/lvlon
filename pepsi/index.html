Three units of work

Each is scoped, fixed-fee and time-boxed. They stack, but none of them assumes the next one.

A

Innovation operating and funding model

The governance layer around the new orchestrator model: how an idea enters, how it gets staged, what kills it, and how the portfolio is balanced between breakthrough bets and near-term productivity. Most corporate innovation models are good at starting things and bad at stopping them, which is where the budget actually goes.

Output
A written operating model, the stage gates with real kill criteria, and the funding mechanics to support them. Yours, in your language, ready to socialise.
Shape
6 to 8 weeks, fixed fee, two milestones
Use when
The model is designed but has not yet met a real portfolio
B

Prototype capacity, composed per mission

A team assembled for one build and stood down after it. Design, engineering and AI, running to the same rule you described: something that works, then iterate. The point is not that we prototype better. It is that your people stop being the constraint on how many bets you can run at once.

Output
A working prototype and the evidence to decide on it, plus a documented handover so the next one does not start from zero
Shape
60 days, fixed fee, milestone-gated

03 //

The fair objection

"We could do a lot of this internally."

You could, and in places you already are. The Leap Network was built on exactly that belief, which is why we trained coaches rather than staying. So this is not a capability argument. It is an arithmetic one, and it is answered by three numbers you already have:

  1. How many prototypes reached a real decision last quarter, and how many problems were waiting behind them?
  2. What share of the person doing the prototyping is spent prototyping, versus finding the problem and keeping people aligned?
  3. When something is killed, how long does the decision take from the moment everyone privately knows?

If those numbers are healthy, you do not need us. If they are not, the constraint is capacity and cadence, not talent, and buying capacity that stands down afterwards is cheaper than carrying it. You keep the ownership and the credit. We are composable, and then we are gone.
