01

Why fwd exists

Advice Stops at the Slide. Engineering Stops at the Spec.

Most enterprises have no shortage of pilots, programs and pressure. What's missing is a mechanism that picks the right problem, builds inside the systems the work actually runs on, measures what changed, and decides honestly whether to continue. Strategy firms hand over the recommendation. Build shops ship to a specification and leave adoption and economics to you. fwd owns both halves, and the result.

Sector doesn't decide fit. The workflow does. High-frequency or labor-intensive, with a baseline somebody already measures, data you can reach, integration risk you can bound, and a decision that lands inside eight to twelve weeks. That profile shows up in operations, service, finance, supply chain and revenue teams across every industry we've worked in.

03

The ten weeks

Four Stages, Four Chances to Stop

The deployment runs on the same stages as everything else we do — Plan, Promote, Produce, Propel — with the decision at each gate agreed before the work starts. Pick a week.

Week ten is readiness for Propel: a working system in one bounded workflow, with the evidence to decide on rollout. It is not an enterprise rollout in ten weeks, and we won't describe it as one.

What lands on day seventy

If the gates were passedA working, security-approved system for one bounded workflow; an auditable evaluation and ROI report; documentation you own; and the next decision stated plainly.

If they weren'tThe documented reasons, the findings that stay useful, the assets worth keeping, and an agreed shutdown. You still own all of it.

Either wayA written postmortem, and a team comfortable telling a sponsor the project should stop.

04

What it takes

What We Need, and What We Turn Down

Embedded work fails for predictable reasons, and nearly all of them are visible before week one. We'd rather find them in the first conversation.

Before week one

Preflight is paid, short, and non-negotiable. Six things have to exist:

Who shows up

A pod stays small on purpose and works next to your growing team, not in place of it. Named people, named counterparts, daily contact, a weekly demo and a fortnightly executive gate.

Forward Deployment Lead

Owns the business outcome. Workflow redesign, stakeholder management, the gate memos. The role the practice is named after.

Technical / AI Lead

Architecture, implementation, evaluation and security. The role we will not start without.

Product / Workflow Engineer

Integration, interface, instrumentation and the adoption detail that decides whether anyone uses it.

Senior review

Partner-level challenge at the gates, at defined hours rather than unlimited availability.

Embedded means operationally embedded — in your systems, your standups and your decisions — with scheduled onsite immersion. It does not have to mean full-time in your building.
