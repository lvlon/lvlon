01

What we'd change

Three Changes, Not Thirty

Your model already routes ideas. These are the three places it bends under pressure, and each is something we have built before.

01

Make "derisked" mean something

Today it's the gate everyone cares about and nobody has defined, so the strongest advocate sets the bar.

What we'd doTurn it into an evidence bar per lens, plus stop rules and a time limit agreed the day a project enters a phase, not the day someone wants out.

Shell. We designed and ran its stage-gated funding model. The hard part was never the funding decision, it was agreeing kill criteria before anyone was invested.

02

Put users inside the gate

Your criteria cover value, architecture, funding and alignment. None of them ask what users did, so Carbonate's research informs decisions rather than making them.

What we'd doAdd a desirability bar to every gate and train teams to clear it, so research carries the same weight as an architecture sign-off.

BCG and Target. Paul founded BCG's human-centered design practice and built Target's Leap Network, training coaches rather than staying: 400+ internal coaches, so the discipline outlived the team that introduced it.

03

Run a program, not just a process

Nothing yet says where good ideas come from, who gets to work on them, or how their time is protected. Innovation at 20–40% of someone's week is where models quietly stall.

What we'd doDesign the program around the process: invited cohorts on set themes, profiles for the people who thrive in small teams, protected capacity, and champions who carry it after we leave.

Target again. 4,500 people through bootcamps, and the network still ran once we were gone.

02

How we plan

We Plan for Decisions, Not Milestones

Most innovation plans promise a straight line. Ours names the choices ahead of time, so every stage collects what the next decision needs.

03

The 14-Point Inspection

Our method, refined across venture builds and corporate programs: fourteen lenses, each with its own sub-questions and an evidence bar that rises gate by gate. It's how "derisked" stops being an opinion. What follows is an illustrative extract. We unpack the full set with your team as part of the engagement.

Example: one lens, part-opened

14 · Evidence & Decision

The lens most stage-gate models leave out: what is proven, what could still kill it, who decides.

Bar at Promote → Produce
  • 01What are the riskiest assumptions still open, and what tests them next?
  • 02What would make us stop, and by when?
  • +4Four more sub-questions, each with the evidence that satisfies it

An example of how one lens works. The remaining thirteen, their sub-questions and the evidence bars get unpacked and tuned with your team during the engagement, then handed over as yours.
